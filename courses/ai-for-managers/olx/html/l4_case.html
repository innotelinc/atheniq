Evidence, Vendors, Accountability

A business case is a decision document. It states what is being asked for, what it costs, what evidence supports the benefit, what could go wrong, and who owns the outcome. If the evidence is weak, the case says so — that is what makes it credible.

The one-page case

  1. Decision requested. Approve a pilot, a purchase, a policy change.
  2. Cost. Licence, implementation, training, review labour, and exit.
  3. Benefit. The measure, the baseline, and the expected change.
  4. Evidence quality. Measured here, measured elsewhere, or vendor claim. Say which.
  5. Risk. The main harms, the controls, and the residual risk.
  6. Owner. The named person accountable for the outcome.

The questions that decide a vendor

  • What data leaves, to where, and under which terms?
  • Is our content used to train models, and can that be turned off in the contract?
  • How long is data retained, and how is deletion evidenced?
  • Which subprocessors are involved, and in which countries?
  • What accuracy claims exist, and what evidence supports them?
  • What does the exit look like: export, deletion, and transition support?

An evasive answer is a finding. Record it, and price the risk it creates.

Accountability does not transfer

The vendor supplies a tool; the organisation still owns the decision the tool informs. If an AI screening feature rejects a candidate, "the model decided" is not an answer a regulator, a customer, or an employee will accept. Name the human owner in the contract review and in the process design.

Try this

Write the one-page case for the change you have been considering, with the evidence quality stated honestly. Then red-team it: what would a finance partner ask, and what would a works council ask? If either answer is "we do not know", that is the next thing to find out.
