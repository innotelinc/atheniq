Measure Before You Change

Process improvement is older than AI and stricter than a demo. Measure the current state, find where the time and the failures actually are, change one thing, and measure again. The tool is the smallest part of the work.

Where the time goes

  • Wait time. Work sitting in a queue or an inbox. Usually the largest share, and AI rarely touches it.
  • Handoffs. Each transfer loses context and adds a delay.
  • Rework. The same case done twice because something was missing.
  • Actual work. The step itself — often the smallest number.

Assistants compress "actual work". If that is 10% of the elapsed time, the ceiling on improvement is small until the queue and rework are addressed.

The net-gain test

A change that saves a step and adds two reviews can be a loss. Write the arithmetic before the pilot: minutes saved per case, multiplied by volume, minus review minutes, minus expected rework. If the result is thin, the change is not ready.

Separate waste from necessary work

Some steps look like waste and are controls: an approval, a check, a record. Removing them to show a speed gain transfers risk to someone else. Remove steps whose only purpose has disappeared; keep the ones that carry accountability, and say which is which.

Vendor claims

"40% faster" usually describes one step in a controlled demo. Ask: faster at what, for which cases, measured against what baseline, including whose review time? A vendor who cannot answer those four questions has given you a marketing number.

Try this

Take a process you own and estimate the four time buckets for a typical case. Then cost the change you are considering, including review and rework. Most managers find the business case depends on the queue, not the assistant.
