Tasks Shift, Roles Change

Managers are usually told to "communicate the benefits". That is not the hard part. The hard part is being honest about which tasks change, what new skill is required, and what happens to the people whose work is most affected.

Talk about tasks, not jobs

"AI will not replace you" is a promise nobody can make about a specific role. "The drafting part of your week shrinks; the checking and client conversation parts grow; here is the training for both" is a statement you can stand behind. It is specific, it names the change, and it gives the person something to act on.

What people need

  • The rule, in one page: what may be assisted, what may not, where to ask.
  • The skill: how to brief, check and correct — with practice, not a slide.
  • The route: an approved tool that works, so the shadow use stops being rational.
  • A voice: a way to report a failure without being blamed for it.

Resistance is information

When an experienced person says the tool will not work, they are often describing an exception the pilot has not met. Ask what case would break it and test that case. The answer is free quality assurance.

Performance during transition

Do not set a productivity target before the training and the review gate exist. People under a target with no approved route will use the fastest unsafe option. Sequence matters: rule, tool, training, then target.

Try this

Write the one-page rule for your team and the three-sentence version you would say in a team meeting. Then ask someone whose work changes most to read it. If they can name what is expected of them and where to get help, the message works.
