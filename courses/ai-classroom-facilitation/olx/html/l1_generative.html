Generative AI in the Classroom

Generative AI is now part of the classroom whether or not it is named. The facilitator's job is to use it deliberately — for material that would otherwise take days — while keeping the judgement, the relationship and the standard in human hands.

What these systems are good at

  • Drafting — a first version of a lesson, an example set, a rubric, a quiz bank.
  • Reformatting — turning a lesson into a handout, a summary, a set of practice questions.
  • Explaining at a level — asking for the same idea at three reading levels or two analogies.
  • Role-play and rehearsal — a learner practising a conversation or an interview.

What they are not

LimitationConsequence for the classroom
ConfabulationA fluent answer can be confidently wrong — verify anything factual
No ground truthIt does not know your syllabus; you supply the source material
Bias in the training dataExamples can skew; review for representation
Non-determinismThe same prompt gives different answers — pin outputs you rely on
Data handlingWhat you paste may leave the institution — never paste learner data

The facilitation stance

  1. Start from the learning outcome, not from the tool.
  2. Generate, then edit. The draft is raw material.
  3. Verify every fact, citation and number before a learner sees it.
  4. Tell learners when and how AI was used — model the transparency you expect.
  5. Keep assessment decisions human.
