Assessment & Ethics

Assessment is where generative AI forces the clearest decisions, and where a facilitator's judgment matters most.

Designing assessment for an AI-present world

  • Assess the process, not only the artefact — a defense, a reflection, a live demonstration, a worked portfolio.
  • Make the task personal — apply the concept to the learner's own context, which no generic answer covers.
  • Assess transfer — a novel situation exposes understanding that a recalled answer does not.
  • Teach and allow the tool where it is allowed — a ban that cannot be enforced teaches nothing; a stated policy does.

Policy and disclosure

State per assessment whether AI use is prohibited, permitted with disclosure, or required. Ask for the disclosure in a form that supports learning — the prompt used, the draft, and what changed — rather than a checkbox. Apply the policy consistently, and never penalise a learner for following it.

Integrity, fairness and bias

  • AI detectors are unreliable; do not make a serious decision on a probability score alone. Investigate, and talk to the learner.
  • Generated feedback can encode bias about names, dialects or backgrounds. Read every automated comment before it reaches a learner.
  • Access is unequal. Anything that assumes a paid model, a fast connection or a personal device must have an institutional alternative.
  • Keep the learner's data out of the model. Assessment records belong in the LMS, not in a chat window.

The educator's non-negotiables

Decisions about a learner's progress are made by a person who can be accountable for them. Use AI to scale feedback and practice; keep judgment, and the responsibility for it, where it belongs.
