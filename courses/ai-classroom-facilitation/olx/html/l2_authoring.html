Authoring with OpenMAIC

OpenMAIC is AthenIQ's multi-agent classroom engine. A classroom is authored as a spec — a scenario, a cast of agents, and the material they work from — and then run live.

The pieces of a classroom

  • Scenario — the situation the session is built around and the outcome it is meant to reach.
  • Agents — the roles participants (or the AI) play: a customer, a site manager, a peer reviewer, a tutor.
  • Source material — the course OLX lesson and any uploaded documents the agents must stay faithful to.
  • Activity — what the learners actually do in the session.
  • Success criteria — how the session is judged to have worked.

Authoring from the courseware

Classroom specs live beside the course, under courses/<slug>/openmaic/classrooms.xml or classrooms.json, so the scenario and the lesson it dramatises are versioned together. The lesson supplies the content; the classroom supplies the situation that makes it matter.

Prompting for material

  1. Give the context — the learner, the level, the outcome.
  2. Give the source — paste the lesson, not the whole syllabus.
  3. Constrain the format — "five questions, one correct answer each, no 'all of the above'".
  4. Ask for the reasoning — why each distractor is plausible.
  5. Iterate — critique the draft and ask for the fix; do not accept the first pass.

Storage and media

Classroom media — recordings, generated assets, learner uploads — is stored on the platform's ONYX object storage through AthenIQ, not written to the container. Keep the classroom's assets under the course's own prefix.

Author the classroom for the session you can actually run: a tight ten-minute simulation with clear roles beats an elaborate scenario nobody has time to brief.
