Network Architecture

Networks are described by their size, their topology, and the layers that carry data. Support work is almost always about one of those three.

By size

  • LAN — one building or site, high speed, locally managed.
  • WAN — links sites together across distance; slower, leased or tunnelled.
  • MAN — a city-scale network, often a campus or metro fibre ring.

Topologies

Modern LANs are switched star topologies: every device connects to a switch, and the switch forwards frames only to the port that needs them. Redundancy comes from a mesh between switches rather than from a physical ring.

Addressing and subnets

192.168.10.0/24 gives 256 addresses; the /24 is the prefix length. A host uses its own subnet for local traffic and the gateway for everything else.

The model that matters

Whether you use OSI's seven layers or TCP/IP's four, the value is the same: diagnose one layer at a time. If the link is up and addressing is valid but names do not resolve, the fault is at the name layer, not the cable.
