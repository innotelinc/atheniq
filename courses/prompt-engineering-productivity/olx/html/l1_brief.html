The Five Parts of a Usable Brief

Most disappointing AI output is not a model problem; it is a brief problem. "Write an email about the delay" leaves the audience, the tone, the facts, and the definition of done entirely to the machine. A brief supplies them.

The five parts

  1. Context. What is happening, for whom, and why now. The model cannot see your project, so the situation has to travel with the request.
  2. Role and audience. "You are writing to a customer whose order is three days late" is a different task from "you are writing to the supplier".
  3. Constraints. Length, tone, reading level, what must not appear, what must be preserved verbatim.
  4. An example. One good output, or one input/output pair, fixes more behaviour than three adjectives. Show the shape you want.
  5. A definition of done. What a reader must be able to do after reading it: decide, reply, approve, act. "Good" is not testable; "answers the three questions in the brief" is.

Vague versus usable

WishBrief
"Summarise this report.""Summarise this report for a shift supervisor who must decide whether to escalate. Six bullets maximum, one number per bullet, quote the page for each number."
"Make it more professional.""Rewrite for a client who has already escalated once. Remove blame, keep the dates, end with the next step and a name."
"Write a plan.""Write a four-week plan for two people, one hour a day, with a checkpoint each Friday and one measurable outcome."

Why the example matters

Adjectives are ambiguous: "concise" means one sentence to one reader and one page to another. An example is unambiguous — it shows the length, the structure, and the level of detail at once. If you have no example of good output, write two short ones yourself; they become reusable assets.

Test the brief before you rely on it

A brief is reproducible when a colleague could run it and get something usable. Check yours by removing your own context: does the request still name the audience, the facts, the constraints, and the finish line? If it only made sense because you were holding the background in your head, the model is holding nothing.

Try this

Take your last three prompts and grade each on the five parts. Rewrite the weakest one and run both versions on the same input. Keep the pair — a before/after is the fastest way to show a colleague why the extra two minutes pay for themselves.
