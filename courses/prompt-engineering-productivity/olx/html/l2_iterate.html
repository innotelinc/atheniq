Critique, Revise, Constrain

A first draft is raw material. The difference between a mediocre result and a usable one is usually two or three focused rounds — not a longer prompt written in advance.

The loop

  1. Critique against criteria. Name what is wrong in specific terms: "the second paragraph answers a question the reader did not ask", "the total is not in the text".
  2. Ask for a revision that fixes only those points. Unbounded "make it better" invites the model to rewrite what was already right.
  3. Constrain the format. If the output must fit a form, a slide, or a three-line reply, say so and show it.
  4. Log the change. One line per round: what you asked, what moved, what broke. The log is how you learn which instruction actually works.

When to stop

Iteration converges, then drifts. Signs you have passed the useful point:

  • The model fixes the named issue but reintroduces one you already fixed.
  • Each round makes the text longer without adding information.
  • You are asking for "more natural" and cannot say what that means.
  • You have stopped checking facts because you are focused on wording.

At that point, take the best draft and edit it yourself. Human editing is cheaper than another round of negotiation, and you keep authorship of the result.

Constrain the shape, not just the words

If the output goes into a fixed structure — a ticket, a status update, a slide — give the structure. A short template in the prompt prevents the reformatting pass later:

Status: green | amber | red
Change since last week: one sentence
Risk: one sentence
Next step and owner: one sentence

Try this

Take one draft and run three rounds, logging each. Then count how many of the fixes survived to the final version. Most learners find the first round does most of the work and the third mostly undoes the second.
