Govern, Map, Measure, Manage

There is no shortage of AI frameworks. The useful question is not which one is authoritative; it is which one your organization can actually run on a Tuesday, with the people and records it has.

The main reference points

FrameworkWhat it gives youWhat it does not do
NIST AI RMFFour functions — govern, map, measure, manage — as a operating loopTell you what is legal in your jurisdiction
OECD AI PrinciplesShared values: human-centred, transparent, accountable, safeProvide an assessment method
EU AI ActA risk-based set of obligations with phased datesApply automatically outside its scope; dates and duties must be checked for your case
Sector rulesHealth, finance, employment, education duties that already bind youCover AI specifically in every case

Frameworks describe practice; law creates obligations. This course teaches the practice and tells you when to ask counsel about the law.

The four functions in one page

  1. Govern. Who decides, who is accountable, what the policy says, what gets recorded.
  2. Map. What the system does, who it affects, where the data comes from, what could go wrong.
  3. Measure. Test the risks you identified; record the evidence, not the intention.
  4. Manage. Accept, mitigate, transfer or stop — with a named owner and a review date.

Do not claim compliance you cannot evidence

"Our product is compliant" is a claim with legal weight. The defensible version is specific: "we maintain a risk register, test the identified harms, record the results, and review quarterly; the legal position for this deployment is being confirmed by counsel." The first sentence invites a claim you cannot support; the second describes evidence.

Try this

Take one AI use in your organization and map it onto the four functions. For each, write what exists today and what is missing. The missing column is your governance backlog, and it will be shorter than the framework documents suggest.
