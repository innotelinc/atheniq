The Questions That Decide the Deal

A vendor review is not a security questionnaire with a tick box at the end. It is the point where you find out whether the contract matches the product demo — and what happens to your data, your users, and your liability after signature.

The seven questions

  1. Data in. What categories are processed, and does the vendor need them all?
  2. Training use. Is our content used to train models? Can that be disabled contractually, not just in a setting?
  3. Retention and deletion. How long, where, and how is deletion evidenced?
  4. Subprocessors and location. Who else touches the data, and in which jurisdictions?
  5. Accuracy and evaluation. What claims exist, on what test set, and what happens when the model is wrong?
  6. Human oversight. What can your staff see, override, and appeal?
  7. Exit. Export format, deletion on termination, and transition support.

The first hour of an incident

  1. Stop the harm: disable the feature or the integration.
  2. Record what happened, when it started, and who is affected.
  3. Tell the owner and the people who need to know — internally first, then externally as required.
  4. Preserve logs before they rotate.
  5. Decide who speaks publicly, and say only what is known.
  6. Fix, then review: what control failed, and what changes before it is re-enabled.

Accountability clause

The contract should say who is responsible for what when the system errs. "The model decided" is not a defence an organisation can offer to a customer, an employee, or a regulator. Name the human owner in the process design, and make the vendor's responsibilities explicit in the agreement.
