A Register Entry That Survives Review

Most risk registers fail in the same way: a score with no harm, a control with no evidence, and an owner who has never seen the entry. A defensible entry is short, specific, and written so the person affected could understand it.

The entry

  1. Use and context. What the system does, for whom, and where it is used.
  2. Affected people. Who bears the consequence: customers, employees, applicants, patients, the public.
  3. Harm. What could go wrong for them — including harm from a wrong denial, not only from a wrong approval.
  4. Likelihood and severity. Justify both, briefly. "Medium" on its own is not a justification.
  5. Controls. What exists, with evidence: a test result, a log, a review record.
  6. Residual risk. What remains after the controls, and who accepts it.
  7. Review date and trigger. When it is revisited, and what change forces an earlier review.

Write the harm, not the adjective

"Reputational risk: medium" tells a reviewer nothing. "An applicant is rejected by a model trained on historical hiring and is never told why; the harm is a lost opportunity and no route to appeal" tells them what to fix. Specific harm statements also make controls obvious.

False comfort

  • Averages hide tails. A low average harm can conceal a severe outcome for a small group.
  • Human in the loop is a control only if the human has the time, the information, and the authority to disagree.
  • Vendor certification is evidence about the vendor, not absolution for your deployment.

Accepting risk

Residual risk is accepted by a named person with the authority to accept it, on a date, in writing. A team cannot accept risk on behalf of the organisation; a manager cannot accept a risk above their delegated limit. If nobody will sign, the answer is not "proceed quietly" — it is "escalate".

Try this

Write one register entry for a real use. Then give it to someone outside the project and ask what they think could go wrong. If they name a harm you missed, the map was incomplete — which is exactly what review is for.
