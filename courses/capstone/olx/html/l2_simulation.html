Running a Service Desk

Beyond fixing one machine, support is a system: a queue, a team, a knowledge base, and a set of commitments. The capstone puts you on that desk.

Managing the queue

  • Work by priority, then age. A P4 waiting three days outranks a P4 that just arrived.
  • Batch ruthlessly. A queue is not a to-do list; group and close the easy wins that unblock people.
  • Watch for an incident hiding in the queue. Ten users reporting the same thing is one incident, not ten tickets.
  • Keep the user informed. Silence is what turns a small delay into an angry ticket.

Escalation that works

A good escalation includes: the symptom, what you tried, the evidence, the impact, and what you need. Never escalate "it's broken" — escalate a decision.

The knowledge base

Every repeat problem is a chance to write an article. The test of a good article: someone with no context can follow it and reach the same result. Writing one article saves more time than resolving one more ticket by hand.

Healthy metrics

MetricWhat it tells you
First-contact resolutionHow much the desk can solve alone
Mean time to resolveHow quickly work completes
Reopen rateWhether fixes actually stuck
Backlog ageWhether the queue is healthy or silently failing

Metrics are a diagnostic, not a whip. If they look bad, find the constraint — often it is access, tooling, or an unwritten process.
