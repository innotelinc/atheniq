Security Fundamentals

Security is part of every support task, not a separate team's concern. The three goals to protect are confidentiality, integrity, and availability.

The threats you will actually see

  • Malware — viruses, trojans, ransomware, and spyware. Ransomware encrypts files and demands payment; it is an emergency.
  • Phishing — email or messages that impersonate a trusted sender to steal credentials or deliver malware.
  • Social engineering — a human being manipulated into breaking procedure. It bypasses technology entirely.
  • Insider risk — accidental or malicious misuse by someone with legitimate access.

Defensive controls

  • Patch promptly. Unpatched software is the most exploited weakness on any network.
  • Endpoint protection. Keep antivirus/EDR installed, updated, and reporting.
  • Least privilege. Day-to-day accounts are not administrators.
  • Backups. A tested, offline backup is the only reliable answer to ransomware.
  • Encryption. Full-disk encryption protects a lost laptop.

If you suspect an infection

  1. Isolate the device from the network — unplug it or disable Wi-Fi. Do not shut it down if the team may need to examine memory.
  2. Do not clean it yourself. Report immediately through the security incident channel and preserve evidence.
  3. Record what you saw, when, and what the user did before it happened.
  4. Do not reconnect or reuse the device until security clears it.

Reporting early is always correct. A support technician who escalates a suspected incident immediately is doing the job well.
