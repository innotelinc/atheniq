Troubleshooting Methodology

Guessing is slow and unsafe. A method turns a vague complaint into an ordered set of tests, each of which eliminates something.

The method

  1. Identify the problem. Gather information: what changed, when did it start, who is affected, can it be reproduced? Question the user without interrogating them.
  2. Establish a theory. Form the most likely and the simplest explanation first. Question the obvious: is it plugged in?
  3. Test the theory. If the test does not confirm it, form a new theory. Do not force the evidence to fit.
  4. Make a plan. Decide the fix and its risk. Confirm backups before anything destructive.
  5. Implement the fix. Change one thing at a time so you know what worked.
  6. Verify. Confirm with the user, check full functionality, and watch for side effects.
  7. Document. Record the cause, the fix, and anything the next technician should know.

Divide and conquer

Test from the middle outward. If a browser cannot load a page, is it the browser, the device, the local network, or the service? A single ping can eliminate half of those. Isolate the layer, then the component, then the setting.

Hardware vs software

Rule out the physical first when it is cheap: a different cable, port, or device. Then move to drivers, configuration, and the application.

When to escalate

Escalate when the problem is beyond your access, affects more than your scope, poses a security risk, or when you have spent your agreed window without progress. Escalation with good notes is a professional decision, not a defeat.
