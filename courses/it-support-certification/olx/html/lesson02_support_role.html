The IT Support Role

IT support is the human interface between technology and the people who depend on it. Your job is not only to fix things — it is to keep people productive while you fix them.

Where support happens

  • Tier 1 — Service desk: first contact. Password resets, account access, basic how-to questions, and triage. Most tickets are resolved here.
  • Tier 2 — Desktop / field support: hands-on diagnosis of hardware, operating systems, and applications that Tier 1 could not close.
  • Tier 3 — Specialists: network, security, server, and vendor engineering for deep or systemic problems.

Escalation moves work to the right tier — it is a success, not a failure, when you pass a ticket on with good notes.

What the role demands

  • Communication: explain a fix in plain language. Avoid jargon unless the user uses it first.
  • Patience and empathy: the person in front of you is usually stressed and has already lost time.
  • Method: work from evidence, not hunches.
  • Integrity: you will hold administrative access. Never look at data you do not need, and never make a change you cannot explain.

Typical tools of the trade

You will meet a ticketing or ITSM system, a remote-support tool, an asset inventory, a knowledge base, and a directory service such as Active Directory or Authentik. Learn the ticketing system first — everything else flows through it.

Professional conduct

Follow the least-privilege principle when granting access, obtain consent before accessing a user's device or files, and record what you changed. A support technician who documents carefully is trusted with more.
