Help Desk Operations

A service desk is a process, not a help button. It exists so problems are solved consistently, measured honestly, and learned from.

The ticket lifecycle

  1. Intake — a request arrives by phone, portal, email, or chat. Capture who, what, when, and the impact.
  2. Categorize & prioritize — urgency (how soon) times impact (how many, how badly).
  3. Diagnose & resolve — work the ticket, document each step.
  4. Escalate when it is beyond your tier or access.
  5. Confirm & close — the user agrees it works, and the fix is written in the ticket.

Prioritization

PriorityExample
P1 — CriticalCompany-wide outage; security incident
P2 — HighAn entire team or key service is down
P3 — MediumOne user cannot work
P4 — LowCosmetic issue, request, or question

Communication

  • Acknowledge quickly, even if the fix takes longer.
  • Give realistic updates and a next checkpoint; never promise what you cannot deliver.
  • Speak to the user's level and avoid blame.
  • Close the loop: tell the user what you changed and how to prevent a repeat.

Knowledge and self-service

Every repeat problem is a chance to write a knowledge-base article or a self-service guide. Documenting the fix so the next person resolves it in half the time is the highest-value work a service desk does.
