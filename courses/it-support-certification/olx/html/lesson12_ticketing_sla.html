Ticketing & SLAs

A ticket is a conversation with a memory. Its quality decides whether the organisation can improve.

A good ticket

  • Summary: one line naming the symptom and the affected system.
  • Impact: who is affected and how badly.
  • Reproduction: the exact steps, and the exact error text.
  • Evidence: screenshots, logs, and timestamps.
  • Actions: what you tried, and the result of each attempt.
  • Resolution: the fix and how the user confirmed it.

"It doesn't work" is not a ticket. "Outlook fails to open with error 0x8004010F after this morning's update; reproduced twice" is.

Service-level agreements

An SLA commits a response and resolution time per priority. Support measures against it:

TermMeaning
Response timeHow soon a human first replies
Resolution timeHow soon the issue is fixed
First-contact resolutionClosed on the first interaction
Reopen rateHow often fixes did not stick

SLAs are targets, not excuses. If you cannot meet one, communicate early rather than silently missing it.

Writing for the next person

Assume the reader knows nothing about this ticket. Future-you, at 2 a.m., is that reader. Clear notes are the difference between a five-minute fix and a five-hour investigation.
