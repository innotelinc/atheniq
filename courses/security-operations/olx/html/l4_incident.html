Incident Response

An incident is a confirmed security event with real impact. Response is a rehearsed process, not improvisation.

The lifecycle

  1. Prepare — playbooks, contacts, access, and backups ready before anything happens.
  2. Identify — confirm the incident and its scope; decide severity.
  3. Contain — stop the spread: isolate hosts, disable accounts, block traffic. Containment buys time.
  4. Eradicate — remove the attacker's foothold: malware, persistence, compromised credentials.
  5. Recover — restore from clean backups, verify, and monitor closely.
  6. Learn — a blameless review that turns the incident into controls and training.

Evidence handling

  • Document who did what, when, and why — in the ticket, as you go.
  • Preserve volatile evidence (memory) before powering a system down, if the team needs it.
  • Keep a chain of custody for any artifact that may become evidence.

Communication

Assign one incident commander and one communications owner. Report facts and uncertainty honestly. Most damage in a botched incident comes from uncoordinated communication, not from the attacker.

The support technician's role

You will often be the first to notice. Isolate, preserve, report, and follow instructions. Do not attempt to clean a compromised system on your own — that is how evidence and time are both lost.
